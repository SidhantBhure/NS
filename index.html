Academic & Educational Security Advisory

Classical ciphers illustrate fundamental cryptographic principles: substitution (confusion), transposition (diffusion), and key spaces. All 8 ciphers are cryptographically obsolete against modern frequency analysis, known-plaintext attacks, and brute-force attacks. They must NEVER be used to protect real-world sensitive data.

Speed ≠ Security

Simple ciphers (Caesar, Vigenère) execute in microseconds, yet are trivially cracked by frequency analysis in milliseconds. Cryptographic strength is measured by key space entropy, diffusion (avalanche effect), and resistance to known-plaintext attacks — not execution speed.
